# BUNSHIK HELL — GAME DESIGN BIBLE
## Part 1: Core Identity & Protagonist

**Status:** Brainstorming / partially confirmed  
**Purpose:** Canonical cross-session context for the Bunshik Hell project.

> **Important:** Confirmed decisions are canonical unless deliberately revised. Proposed/open ideas remain flexible.

---

# 1. Game Identity

## Title
# BUNSHIK HELL

## Genre
2D action-platformer / survival-horror / boss-focused arcade game.

## Primary inspirations

- **Super Mario** — readable platforming, accessible movement, exploration, and game feel.
- **Cuphead** — theatrical boss battles, hand-drawn cartoon energy, exaggerated animation, and strong visual character.
- **Little Nightmares** — vulnerability, environmental storytelling, unsettling scale, disturbing creatures, and horror through the perspective of a small protagonist.
- **Evil Dead / Re-Animator-style horror** — absurd, over-the-top vintage horror, grotesque transformations, excessive cartoon gore, and horror that can become ridiculous.

## Setting

An increasingly nightmarish version of **Korean 분식 (bunsik / Korean snack-food and street-food) culture**.

Food becomes:
- Characters
- Enemies
- Bosses
- Hazards
- Weapons
- Environmental objects
- Gameplay systems
- The logic of the world itself

## Desired emotional mixture

**Cute → strange → funny → disturbing → grotesque → absurd.**

The goal is not “Korean Cuphead,” but:

> **A bizarre food-horror universe that could only be Bunshik Hell.**

---

# 2. The Three Design Pillars

## Pillar 1 — FUN TO PLAY
Movement, jumping, combat, environmental interaction, and boss encounters must feel satisfying independently of the artwork.

## Pillar 2 — ABSURD FOOD
Food is the game's fundamental material. Food should have physical and behavioral rules based on what the food actually is, exaggerated into cartoon-horror logic.

## Pillar 3 — CARTOON THEATER
Characters and environments behave like exaggerated performers. Bosses make entrances, enemies have distinctive reactions, deaths can be spectacular, and animation is expressive and theatrical.

---

# 3. Horror Philosophy

Bunshik Hell is not straight horror. It is **horror viewed through absurd cartoon logic**.

The game can contain:
- Grotesque food creatures
- Disturbing environments
- Unsettling sounds
- Strange body deformation
- Excessive cartoon gore
- Familiar food behaving in impossible ways
- Things moving that should not be alive
- Vintage-horror-style grotesquery

Desired reaction:

> “That's actually kind of horrifying.”

followed by:

> “…why is that so funny?”

The game should be cute enough to invite the player in, strange enough to make them uncomfortable, funny enough to make them laugh, and grotesque enough to make them wince.

---

# 4. The Protagonist

## The 떡

The player controls **one single 떡 (ddeok / rice cake)**.

It is not a bowl, plate, or humanoid food character.

It is:

> **One single piece of 떡 with sauce dripping off its body.**

### Confirmed physical design

- One single 떡
- No arms
- No legs
- Sauce dripping from its body
- Soft/elastic physical appearance
- Upper portion behaves like a head-like area without necessarily being a separate literal head

The lack of limbs is deliberate.

> **The 떡 should never behave like a humanoid with its limbs removed.**

It has its own physical method of movement.

---

# 5. Protagonist Personality

The 떡 is:

- Curious
- Nervous
- Shy
- Inquisitive
- Ignorant of what is happening
- Easily unsettled
- Not naturally heroic
- Persistent despite being frightened

It does not necessarily understand the world.

Its attitude is closer to:

> “…What is happening?”

followed by:

> “…Okay. I guess I'm going this way.”

The player discovers the world alongside the protagonist.

This creates an important horror device:

> **The player may understand the horror before the 떡 does.**

Its reactions should often be:
- Hesitation
- Curiosity
- Recoiling
- Looking
- Cautious approach
- Sudden panic
- Continuing onward anyway

---

# 6. Movement System

The 떡's body is soft and elastic.

It moves through **deformation rather than conventional walking or running**.

## Fundamental rule

> **The 떡 does not move like a humanoid.**

There are:
- No legs pumping
- No arms swinging
- No conventional walking cycle
- No conventional running cycle

Its entire body is the locomotion system.

## Basic locomotion

The upper/head-like portion:

1. Lurches backward
2. Bends/compresses
3. Squiggles forward
4. Pulls the body along
5. Repeats

The result is an unusual, elastic, inchworm-like movement.

The character should feel like a piece of food that has somehow learned to move rather than a human character disguised as food.

---

# 7. Jumping

Jumping is an exaggerated version of the normal locomotion mechanism.

## Jump sequence

**Bend backward → compress → rapidly straighten → launch**

While airborne:
- The 떡 becomes slightly elongated
- The body can appear floppy or elastic
- Sauce can continue to move/drip with the body

On landing:

**SQUASH**

Then the 떡 quickly recovers.

The jump should visually communicate that the 떡 is launching itself using its own elasticity.

---

# 8. The “Head”

The 떡 does not necessarily have a separate literal head.

Instead, its upper section behaves like one.

It communicates emotion through posture and deformation.

- **Curious:** leans forward
- **Frightened:** pulls backward
- **Confused:** tilts or squiggles uncertainly
- **Interested:** slowly creeps toward something
- **Startled:** sudden full-body recoil
- **Nervous:** small repeated movements or hesitant forward motion

Body deformation is both **movement and acting**.

---

# 9. Combat Philosophy

Combat should not transform the 떡 into a conventional action hero.

The protagonist is physically small and vulnerable.

It survives through:
- Its elastic body
- Sauce
- Environmental objects
- Understanding enemy behavior
- Avoidance
- Opportunistic use of the environment

The game should repeatedly ask:

> **Can I fight this?**  
> **Can I manipulate it?**  
> **Can I use the environment?**  
> **Can I make something else kill it?**  
> **Or should I just run?**

This supports the Little Nightmares influence while retaining action-platformer gameplay.

---

# 10. Current Combat Categories

## A. Body Combat

The 떡 weaponizes its own body.

### Roll Attack
The 떡 compresses and launches itself into a roll. It is effective against certain enemies but not universally effective.

### Bounce / Jump Attack
The 떡 jumps onto suitable enemies. It can squash certain soft/crispy enemies and bounce away from some tougher food enemies.

Enemy reactions are governed by food physics rather than a universal rule.

## B. Sauce Combat

### Sauce Flick
A targeted sauce attack directed at a specific target.

### Sauce Spin
The 떡 spins and flings sauce around itself, hitting things in the surrounding area.

Sauce is not a universal damage projectile. Different foods can be damaged, unaffected, absorb it, or have special reactions.

Lit cigarettes are extinguished by sauce.

## C. Environmental Combat

Environmental combat is **essential**, not a secondary gimmick.

The player can potentially:
- Throw toothpicks
- Throw or knock objects
- Push objects
- Trigger hazards
- Drop objects onto enemies
- Manipulate cooking equipment
- Use environmental hazards to defeat enemies

Possible objects include toothpicks, chopsticks, skewers, spatulas, pots, bowls, plates, and other kitchen utensils.

---

# 11. Avoidance as a Core Mechanic

Not every enemy should be intended to be killed.

Some encounters should reward:
- Running
- Hiding
- Manipulating the environment
- Distracting enemies
- Luring enemies into hazards
- Simply getting past them

The player should sometimes think:

> **“I don't think I'm supposed to fight this thing.”**

That feeling is desirable.

---

# 12. Emerging Food-Physics Principle

A major design principle:

> **Food should obey food logic.**

Different foods respond differently to the 떡's attacks.

Examples:
- Tall 어묵 can be knocked over by a rolling 떡.
- Soft 만두 can be jumped on and squashed.
- Small 뻔데기 can be jumped on and squashed.
- Noodle-based 라면 can absorb sauce and remain unharmed.
- A lit cigarette can be extinguished by sauce but hurts the 떡 if jumped on.
- 튀김 can become damaged, broken, crumbled, and eventually soggy.

Avoid treating every enemy as:

> Enemy + HP bar + generic damage.

Instead:

> **Enemy material + physical properties + food behavior = gameplay rules.**

---

# 13. Current Confirmed Enemy Rules

## 어묵
- Tall food enemy
- The 떡's roll attack is effective
- A successful roll knocks the 어묵 dead
- Its tall/rigid physical form explains the reaction

## 라면
- Difficult enemy
- Primarily an avoidance encounter
- Sauce is absorbed with no damage
- Conventional combat should not be an effective solution
- The player generally needs to find a way around or past it

## 만두
- Common smaller enemy
- Can be jumped on
- Jumping can squash/kill it
- Functions similarly to a basic platform-game enemy

## 뻔데기
- Common small enemy
- Can be jumped on and squashed
- Intended to appear in groups/common encounters
- Another basic enemy type with a more grotesque food-horror presentation

## Lit Cigarettes
- Essential enemy type
- Can initially resemble an environmental object
- Sauce extinguishes them
- Jumping on a lit cigarette hurts the protagonist
- Can create deceptive environmental danger

---

# 14. 튀김 Design Rules

튀김 is a **family of enemies**, not a single creature design.

Possible forms:
- Shrimp
- Sweet potato
- Squid
- 김말이
- Vegetable fritters
- Other fried foods

## Size and durability

Size determines durability/HP.

- **Small 튀김:** low durability
- **Medium 튀김:** moderate durability
- **Large 튀김:** high durability

Size should naturally communicate relative difficulty.

---

# 15. 튀김 Combat Reactions

## Sauce Flick
Effective, but not an immediate kill.

Damage is visibly represented as progressive deterioration:

**Fresh → broken → crumbling → soggy/dead**

## Roll Attack
Damages 튀김.

## Jump Attack
Damages 튀김, and the 떡 bounces off afterward.

This can allow 튀김 to function as dangerous temporary platforms.

## Environmental Attacks
Environmental attacks can kill 튀김 in one hit.

Examples:
- Falling pots
- Falling pans
- Falling spatulas
- Other sufficiently heavy objects

## Boiling Water
Boiling water kills 튀김.

## Fryers
Fryers are **not death traps for 튀김**.

They are where 튀김 come from.

A fryer can function as:
- A 튀김 spawning point
- An environmental element
- A source of escalating enemy encounters
- Part of a puzzle/combat scenario

A fryer might produce:

**튀김 → 튀김 → 튀김 → 튀김**

The player realizes the fryer itself is an enemy source.

---

# 16. Environmental Ecosystem

The environment should increasingly function as a **food ecosystem** rather than a collection of arbitrary hazards.

Examples:

**Fryer → produces 튀김**

**Boiling water → kills 튀김**

**Sauce → extinguishes cigarettes**

**Ramyeon → absorbs sauce**

**Tall 어묵 → can be toppled by rolling**

Level design, enemy design, puzzle design, and combat design can therefore overlap.

The player should discover relationships between:
- Food
- Cooking methods
- Hazards
- Objects
- Enemy behaviors
- The protagonist's abilities

---

# 17. Current Design Philosophy

A strong emerging principle is:

> **The player should learn how the food world works by experimenting.**

The game should reward questions such as:
- “What happens if I sauce this?”
- “Can I jump on this?”
- “Can I roll through this?”
- “Can I throw something at it?”
- “What happens if I lure it over there?”
- “Can the environment kill it?”
- “Why did that food react differently?”

The result should feel like discovering the bizarre rules of a living food ecosystem.

---

---

# 19. North-Star Test

When evaluating a proposed feature, ask:

> **Does this feel like something only Bunshik Hell would do?**

And:

> **Does this preserve the idea that the 떡 is a small, strange, limbless piece of food trying to survive a horrifying world it doesn't fully understand?**

If not, reconsider the feature.

---

# 20. Core Identity in One Paragraph

> **Bunshik Hell is a grotesque, absurd 2D action-platformer inspired by Super Mario, Cuphead, Little Nightmares, and over-the-top vintage horror. The player controls a single limbless 떡 with sauce dripping from its body. It does not walk or fight like a humanoid; it bends, squiggles, compresses, and launches itself through a nightmare version of Korean 분식 culture. The 떡 is curious, nervous, shy, and largely unaware of why everything around it is horrifying. Combat combines elastic body attacks, sauce, environmental objects, and avoidance. Enemies follow their own food-based physical rules, creating a strange ecosystem where an 어묵 can be toppled by a roll, a 만두 can be squashed, a 라면 may be impossible to fight, cigarettes can be extinguished with sauce, and 튀김 emerge from fryers and can be ruined through repeated attacks or destroyed by boiling water and falling objects. The game's central identity is the collision of cute food, grotesque horror, absurd comedy, theatrical animation, and physical food logic.**
